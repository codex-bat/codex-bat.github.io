romance

📅 September 9, 2026

I was so unprepared for this. some days ago I watched a video about the male gaze (wtf am I having on my youtube front page). in it, the person contrasts overly sexualized "male gaze" moments in any random film specifically with Portrait of a Lady on fire and IT'S SO FUCKING OVER-PRAISED (you just got an "oh" moment, didn't you? and you just smiled being identified --- OR you're upset with me for stating that and no longer engage with me emotionally. either is fine --- I'm being so pretentious right now anyway). it's ridiculous. the video talked about how there's this "intense romance" with no over-sexualization or any at all whatsoever and yet still "so profound" and "deep" and r/im14andthisisdeep (they didn't say that, now I am).

why was I so stupid to believe all of that? holy...

even those reddit threads that agree with me when I say that the characters are entirely flat with little to no depth (and many other glaring issues I might get into later): they still praise its cinematography. I don't. having several wideshot, distant shots of characters in the background as we're shown the natural earth feels like an exploitation of the very earth they stand on. in a sci-fi film, it makes total sense: it's a flex of technology and part of world-building, but even there it is VERY OFTEN cheap without having a purpose and just blindly showing random flashy imagery. even in natural settings, showing the landscape, showing just nature is perfectly fine and respectable in art, BUT WITH A THEME. WITH A REASON. WITH AN IDEA. blindly showing a "romantic interest" --- if you can call it that --- far away does nothing. there're no hinted emotions in the scene, not with the soundtrack, not with the intentionality of the camera placement... it's movement, lighting... anything. you're left there to project your own emotions onto the empty canvas --- which is a very empty one.

it's rare and should be praised when the shot is finally a close-up of her hands... which doesn't tell me much. she has one hand over the other... am I missing something? is there some gothic meaning to it? the film is about feminism, maybe there's some culture I'm missing. maybe not. I cannot tell, because a lot of things are just random. Sophie (maid/maiden) is the only character me and redoubt both agreed was the most lively and obviously through some hardship. the two "lead actresses" (yeah, right) are lifeless dolls. Honestly, Sophie reminded me just a tiny bit of Zero from The Grand Budapest Hotel! The others... well, just forget about them.

and you know what? it's my bad for expecting good romance in this film, good representation of anything at any capacity at all whatsoever. the gathering at around the mid-section of the film was weird and cult-like (though the song they sang there was definitely the only highlight of the film for me). by that point, I seriously thought these women were trying to be some sort of witches. I kept trying to guess where and when we are. where in France is there a villa on some random land/island with women running the whole place? I haven't seen not one single man. if it was supposed to be a feminist film, why not show some opposition? some doubt? or the obvious hardship? idk... justify anything that's happening in the film. Sohpie isn't feeling well after being spread wide by some "old witch lady"-looking person (apparently that was her getting an abortion. I only found that out from reviews. what the fuck. I was so confused for the majority of the movie) and isn't asleep yet. so the lead characters somehow decide to paint her in that "giving birth" position...? even though she, assumingly, wasn't feeling well? isn't that just... child porn? wtf? when having her "abortion", Helaise (I don't have the right alphabet for this, sry) tells our protagonist --- who looks away in disgust, discomfort, shame... not sure --- to "look at the event" as Sophie gets her abortion ig. it's uncomfortable and weirdly... cultish --- especially if this film truly is feminist. "LOOK, OBSERVE THIS GROTESQUE SCENE. BELIEVE IN OUR CAUSE!"

now look, there are a bunch of disturbing and terrible films made by men too, clearly. A LOT. but I don't watch those and I didn't think PoALoF (acronym of film title) was trying to compete with that.

so the characters were flat. they were dull. they were lifeless. they were constantly in awkward, uncomfortable and emotionally restrained suspense --- not tension, simply just... stillness. and suddenly, out of absolutely-fucking-nowhere around the mid-section of the film, when our protagonist gets her extension on her painting, their dynamic changes entirely and they talk more like never before. they couldn't laugh or do ANYTHING in a week and suddenly they're besties from nowhere. it was forced --- not even, it was simply wrong. it felt like two separate books stitched together with nothing at all. dialogue was messy and the phrasing of the script was often awkward. the arguments between the lead acters was ridiculous and artificial. they were constantly saying out-loud each others thoughts like they knew it when I, myself, had no idea what either of them were feeling, because they had NO EMOTIONS AT ALL. Helaise says to our brunette that she touches her forehead when she doesn't know what to say, breaths through her mouth when overwhelmed, etc. --- but none of that was obviously established. it was random. all camera shots were so distant that I didn't get to see any of it.

this isn't the only issue with arguments. I thought it was only the translation (as this is a french film) that was lagging behind or messing up, but redoubt knows French quite, very well. she didn't need subtitles and yet both of us were equally as appalled by some of the grammar or sometimes the dialogue entirely. our protagonist gets her period. Sophie gives her a suggestion on smt that could help. Sophie says she "hasn't had her period"... but then she's asked if she's gotten it more than once and she says "three times", followed by the fact that it's her "first time" or such... what?! I'm not an imbecile --- if you're here, you probably know that by now --- but goddamn, throughout most of the duration of this piece of "art" I was utterly lost again and again. oh and then there's the Hades mythology creeping in. Sophie rightfully points out how unreasonable it was for the spouse to turn around to see their wife. totally agree. Sophie is the only decent character ever. but our painter reinterprets it as the man "choosing the memory of her instead, and choosing the poet's choice" or something as such. no, absolutely not. choosing one single moment of her is selfish and frankly not what love --- NOR LUST --- is/would be. he could've had an entire life with her to gain "memories" of her and they could've died on their own terms. but that's a critique unrelated to the movie, only held to it by the fact it was mentioned and by the fact our characters rationalized it poorly.

Helaise is madly uncultured. she's only been exposed to "organ music", they call it. and she says it's bleak --- that was a powerful disrespect to me. I grew up with church music. I've heard the organ my whole childhood. I got tired of it too, but I could never not respect how magnificent and majestic those large, looming pipes are and how articulate their sound is. I now occassionally compose organ plays digitally. it's not the same at all, yet playwright --- who was the Kanthor playing in the church --- is always impressed by how I manage to materialize the same majestic sound (with similar progressions) she was always fascinated by. Helaise's dissatisfaction with "organ music" is deeply insulting and ironically quite close-minded.

one of the few beautiful moments to me was the fact during Sophie's sewing scenes (two scenes in the film; that's it. that's all) goes from having a blossoming flower pot next to her, to a slightly withered one after our leads' goofy non-sensical argument.

and look, I like being logical. yes. a lot. so I tried excusing the characters' awkward mannerisms to the times they lived in (which, we assumed was about the 18th century; very likely, especially because of Vivaldi), but it was so glaringly painful and there were so many things I just simply couldn't excuse. I'm pretty sure I even saw a lady with a thermostat in the group setting.

circling back to my logical tendencies: I still expected romance. I expected to feel something. I can feel things. The good doctor has been consistently pissing me off AND making me cry simultaneously. the writing got worse by now (we just finished Season 6 with forlorn yesterday), and yet the emotional weight is still all there. almost perfectly intact. and especially with The Lake House, I balled my eyes out so hard. but with POALOF, there was nothing to grab on to. there was no intimate romance. there was just empty, random and horny sex. this is anything, but feminist. idk what it IS.

and btw this is a story about a painter's job-time fling and yet the movie isn't even shown in the lenses of a painter. I would bet CS (director) doesn't paint. there's no proof or showcase of a hyperfixation on any part of Helaise as she's being painted at all ever. no close-ups of her face when our protagonist is observing her on their beach walks as a representation of our prot memorizing and admiring Helaise's facial features intimately so she could draw her. this is especially why I was expecting close-ups. you cannot draw or paint AND NOT SEE THE DETAILS INTIMATELY. I would know. I used to draw as an escapism throughout my teenage years. I wasn't as good at it as others might believe or claim, but I knew how to draw. drawing was intimate. now, I tried painting too, but controlling the wet paint itself was too much mental gymnastics for my brain. being a messy, grey-fingered pencil-shading kid fit me more.

so that's that. a movie/film that doesn't really deserve its MASSIVE FUCKING PRAISE holy shit. "best film in 2019", yikes... was the bar that low in that specific year? this film failed on so many layers for me. It has to be that anyone who "loved" it, was just projecting... at face-value... there's nothing to admire. you can only admire yourself in it, because you force yourself onto it --- which then speaks of your character and then yeah... clearly this movie isn't for me.

I hope this didn't ruin our connection; hope to see you again soon :3

edit: in this post I was repeatedly too vauge. characters aren't "lifeless" or "doll-like", because they don't say enough. the acting is horrid too. as in: I see no hidden expressions or quiet moments. there are no quiet moments. literal silence? yes. quiet reflection? no; just pretentious gaps in storytelling and I'm sorry for making that sound like a bot (literally the saem cadence and everything. ew). Sophie didn't say much either, yet she felt far more life-like, because we could see her hurt in her restraint. she is a maid. she has to act appropriately, but you can see that she was a traumatized kid (especially now knowing that she had an abortion (again, super not obvious)). and let's not forget the literal depiction of child intimacy, alright?

again: I'm fine with characters not talking or not expressing their emotions. but you can "not talk" without failing basic storytelling. Wall-E is my favourite movie ever and I got bored of the first act of it as a kid, but I still loved it and needed that first act. it was still very beautiful and telling. the restraint in everything in this movie feels punishing and alienating, even when the characters themselves show something else enitrely. they show us they are having sex, they're exploring each other and yet we're still too far away from them. we're shown Helaise's (I feel like I'm writing her name wrong...) breasts so non-chalantly around three thirds of the way in as if it was nothing. there's nothing valuable or meaningful. the only "strong" moment is when it turns out that this was all just a fling (as if it wasn't obvious) and get butthurt, when their chemistry and relationship was never even properly established. the circumstances aren't impossible, because the world around them is holding them back, the film NEVER establishes that. instead, we're given enough evidence to deduce that the only thing impossible here is their love for each other. I tried arguing that we can assume the world is oppressive by the assumed time we're in, but that doesn't justify it enough when the film constantly shows us women being helped out by men. the only "oppressive power" is some anonimous man that Helaise is being wed to by HER MOTHER. she can say no. she can stand up for herself. there's nothing around her that could be stopping her except her own inapt behaviour. I only saw a supportive and expressive women's group singing freely and a large, empty palace. there's nothing holding anyone back, only themselves.

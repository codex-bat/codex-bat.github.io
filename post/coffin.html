coffin

📅 Oct 28, 2026

knowing me... that title probably gave it away.

but first things first: a title cannot be a title without it serving as a double-edged sword... so— forlorn, thank you for your 5 years... take care, even if.... you showed the coldest side of you in our last conversation. I never thought all of it could die, but here it is.

now, onto what this dreg is actually about: TCOAAL (Andy and Leyley)... I found this game before it even came out and got really curious and intrigued by its premise. it was a very unique dark romance narrative that I rarely see openly and explicitly liked or cherished (ik, ew me). and I must say, as I played the game I saw a lot of familiar things from my childhood, my life, my once-friends' lives and their childhoods... so on. and some parts of the game were close to hitting home, though I struggled to really connect with the narrative (this is not true for everything. lately I've been on a crying spree over Suits, dawg help me).

but here's the thing... this game was "released" in 2023, but it's still in early access and while there would be nothing wrong with that, the current state of the game is... ehhhhh

I liked this game... really! but let's just look at things more clearly: the story didn't really evolve at all from its demo that I played back then. the characters act basically the same and it feels like despite being "given choices" I don't actually have any. I played through the episodes and I only got 3-4 achievements out of 20. most of the other achievements seem to meet certain criteria to try and encourage replayability, except it fails at that, because I have no intentions on replaying it. just saving once for one branching choice seems like a chore, because a large portion of the narrative or dialogue is the same. and the dialogue is mostly the same. it's always "Leyley says this, Andy gets annoyed by that. inner comments. leyley inner comments. blablabla annoyed annoyed annoyed" and it gets boring fast. and if the narrative is the whole point and I'm feeling compelled to skip the whole dialogue over and over again, because it's not useful or they're not saying anything... then what's the point? oh, ik! the gameplay is the point! except wait... no, it's not. the gameplay is the same repetitive "he told what to do with not at all subtle dialogue then get this item, get that item" and then finish the "puzzle"... or wander around and get lost if you don't know how to read the obvious instructions. and it's always the same. nothing ever changes.

and about the branching narrative: is it really branching?? most of it seems like it leads to the same road except one that is more obviously incestuous, which has an "Episode 3: Burial" not even finished. nice job. in the early access disclaimer, the " developer" said she'd be pushing updates in episodes... but it looks like they're pushing updates in "episode fragments" instead. it's like an episode in a random show except it's supposed to have several plotlines running in parallel and the publisher only got one or two of them out for now (it's downgrading).

I get that this is an indie game, but you're telling me the "developer" gets to throw her money around to legally go after "bad actors" and for coming up with a "guideline on how to produce fan-products on the IP" and I should consider that to be her acting in good faith? are you joking? I have 9.6hrs on the game and I don't feel especially compelled to "read", " watch" or "play" more, because I feel I already know where it's going. I get that the art must be what the bulk of the time is going towards, but seriously can we not afford to give the game any more versatility or better storytelling? we see Andrew and Ashley doing heinous things and Andrew trying to grasp for a solution to their very real problem while Ashley lives in a more abstract realm. as the scenes change and the "story unfolds" the only change is the problem at hand that needs a solution, not much else — and that leads to you being told exactly what you need to do. and if you consider to think freely (such as, realizing you need to put baking soda before vinegar to release a clog in a shower or toilet) the choice you're given leads to the same result anyway... so why even bother...? and the asides from one character to another or the "fourth-wall"-breaking episode name-outs start becoming shallow and cheap real quick.

and why don't emotions land? because we, the player, are shown their backstory, their memories and then get put back into the present where we see no reflection in them at all whatsoever. I understand they're horrible people, but what's the point of showing us the reason why they're dysfunctional if we don't actually see them struggle in that dysfunction? don't get me wrong, every character (except the dad) is clearly miserable beyond repair, but not once did I see any of them lash out with actually valid and grounded emotions. none of them can handle or comprehend their own emotions and it's so annoying, it's almost like it's written by an author who, themselves, doesn't know or understand the way emotions or past experiences can impact someone. and that's what makes the storytelling feel so infantile. in the 9 hours I've played, it hasn't matured enough. not really. the cannibalism is also — like everything else — on the nose. we chop up the guy at the beginning then eat him. oh guess what, we'll do it to our parents as well and instead of hiding the body or leaving, we cook it into a nicely made soup and feed ourselves. yeah, ofc none of those "friends and neighbours" came knocking in that 1-2hrs that it must've taken to make a soup from scratch (never mind managing the bodies and everything that comes with cleaning afterwards); it's the coffin game, after all. and who would think to eat their parents first thing's first? especially if Andrew is still disgusted by it? I mean where's the logic...? c'mon... it's very typical of a badly written wattpad story, sadly. and it's unfortunate, because the dynamic really did seem unique when I was first exposed to it, but then it just... fell apart (because things didn't change and instead the storybeats became gags).

going back to my criticism on the author's legal ventures; this is (apparently) what she said about the intended audience: "The target demographic is women who enjoy true crime shows, gothic romance, and dark comedy. I've no idea who showed up though, and it doesn't matter. Everyone is welcome to give me money!"

there are multiple things wrong with that though:
1. I might not be a woman, but that shouldn't change the fact that I enjoy true crime and this isn't... really... that. just because Andrew keeps blabbering on about how they'll hide a body, it doesn't suddenly make it true crime. perhaps what she meant was "somewhat gruesome and bloody... like in true crime".
2. is it really dark comedy or dumb comedy? a few suicidal jokes doesn't make it "comedy".
3. Let's get to the meaty parts: " I don't know who showed up though" sure you do. there are a lot of weirdos and people with questionable fetishes, but she's trying to avoid accountability. now instead of everyone being "welcome", she's going to "protect her IP" even though she doesn't even have a finished game of it yet — way to establish the IP as a AAA-company wannabe.
4. "everyone is welcome to give me money" that's the thing... is the legal parkour about the IP or money? apparently it's abt "mirror sites" and "illegal merchandise", but like... really? I doubt any of this is necessary. here's a stellar contrast of that: team cherry never had an IP guideline that restricted their community from certain things, they just stated a few things in their FAQ, but it was always open. instead, they just limited the community from mass production — effectively gatekeeping it from corporations.

nemlei, on the other hand, didn't say " corporate entities" or "mass producers". no, she specifically used the phrasing "bad actors" in "as the amount of bad actors in the fandom grows", which almost directly points me in the direction of r34 NSFW works... and I mean, really? a smut author writing about non-shy incest and cannibalism is surprised that the community is lusting over it?

I know I shouldn't be offended by this and instead disgusted that there are erotic artists making incestuous NSFW — although... in fact, I'm holding myself back with gorilla arms not to explicitly state I'm a degenerate —, but the "Protecting the Work" devlog/news on the game's forum was really unexpected to me. it left a bad taste in my mouth. just because the "developer" was open about their greed, it doesn't make this any better...

well, no matter. ty for reading this! I sure do appreciate your time here... hope I didn't cause you any distress today
